Every rule below is Kevin's (or Joe Sugarman's) own, then what it means for our clips, captions and DMs.

## 1. Believe it, or it doesn't sell
Kevin: a big company copied his infomercial 100% - same set, a look-alike host, every word transcribed - and it
failed. "The secret ingredient ... was a person who was selling a product that they 100 percent believed in ...
You hired an actor ... What was missing was the energy."
**For us:** the voice in the caption is a real student who uses Kevin's material (Kamay), never a narrator
pretending. No AI clone of Kamay, no actor. When Kamay appears on camera, it is him, saying what he believes.

## 2. Don't sell. Improve the person's life
"I wasn't trying to sell my product ... I was trying to improve the quality of people's lives." To the speaker he
trained: "You want to sell ... You have to forget that. You have to think about what's best for this person."
**For us:** every caption gives something useful on its own, even to someone who never comments. The gift line
says what the audios DO for the viewer, not what we want from them.

## 3. Facts tell, stories sell
Kevin, Ep. 28: "facts tell, stories sell." His $450M Mega Memory show was a story plus a live demonstration.
**For us:** the planner prefers arcs where Kevin tells what happened (a person, a place, a number) over arcs where
he lists principles. The caption retells the story's setup in 2-3 lines and stops before the ending.

## 4. Show proof, live
The Mega Memory show: Kevin names 25 strangers in the audience, then recalls 15 random items in order, on camera.
**For us:** clips where Kevin demonstrates or gives a checkable fact (a date, a dollar figure, a named person) go
first. The hook carries the proof ("$250,000 in an hour", "25 names, no notes").

## 5. Ask, don't tell (the 2-step formula)
Step 1: be genuinely interested in the other person. Step 2: ask - "what, when, where, who, how and why", then
"Why is that?" and "Tell me more." Larry, a shy car salesman, let customers sell themselves by asking "Why is that?"
**For us:** the caption's closing question asks about the VIEWER's life, specifically ("What would you pay off
first?"), never "What do you think?". Comment replies by hand follow the same rule: ask one question back.

## 6. Make yes easy
Joe Sugarman's toll-free number: "the response was enormous. We sold out by noon." Remove every step between
wanting and having.
**For us:** one keyword, first line, the gift arrives in their messages in seconds. Never a link they must copy,
never two steps where one works.

## 7. Duplicate the pattern, not the copy
Kevin: "Duplicate success, follow patterns of success ... Most people try to reinvent the wheel." Yet the exact
copy of his show failed (rule 1).
**For us:** take the PATTERN of what wins (money topics, plain-claim hooks, keyword first line - see PLANNING.md
"What wins on TikTok") and make it in our own voice and look. Never a frame-for-frame copy of another account.

## 8. Attitude before technique
Kevin's "training balance scale": attitude/vibration on one side, technique on the other; "it's your attitude,
not your aptitude, that determines your altitude."
**For us:** mix in clips where Kevin teaches how rich people THINK, not only what they do - roughly 1 in 3.

## 9. People love to buy, but hate being sold (v6zdmb0)
Kevin on his first infomercial: everyone on TV would "look into the camera and sell, sell, sell ... So what I said
is people love to buy, but they hate being sold." He made a show where "nobody feels like they're being sold",
and good advertising "is genuine and authentic ... people don't feel like they're being sold."
**For us:** Kamay's own rule in Kevin's words. No hype words ("insane", "life-changing", "you need this"), no
countdowns, no fake scarcity. The clip teaches; the gift is offered once, plainly.

## 10. Teach and sell at the same time
At a seminar with Zig Ziglar, Brian Tracy and Charlie "Tremendous" Jones, who each taught for two hours and then
sold for 30-45 minutes, Kevin outsold them "two to one combined": the interviewer's words, "you were teaching and
selling simultaneously ... nobody felt like they were being sold."
**For us:** the gift belongs INSIDE the lesson, not after it: the caption shows how the 14 audios continue
the same idea Kevin just taught in the clip, not a separate ad tacked on. Only claim what the audios really
contain (offers.json); never invent a lesson number or a link between them.

## 11. Use every channel on the same people
His Guru Kev book sold by email for six months; then a direct-mail piece to the SAME list sold again. "Email
marketing is not dead and neither is Facebook."
**For us:** a person who commented once is the warmest audience we have. Today they get the gift and nothing
else; a second useful touch (a follow-up message, the link page) is a build for later, with Kamay's yes - never
promised in a caption before it exists.

# Hooks and story - the patterns the planner works from

Adapted for Kamay from the master content prompt he was taught (Daniel's workshop, Sept 2026),
plus what this account's own numbers have proven. Our hook is **two short lines, six words total**,
burned on the clip for the first 3 seconds, so every pattern below is compressed to that size.

## What the numbers said on 23 Sept 2026 (n=62, see MASTERY.md)
- On TikTok, `What...` openers beat `Why...` beat `How...` by a wide margin. On Instagram "How" still works. YouTube ignores the hook entirely - it is capped by retention.
- Saying **rich / wealthy** (when Kevin really is talking about them) is the single strongest word we have measured.
- A **number** in the hook helps: the best hook to date is "The 30% rule every wealthy person follows".
- A hook with no concrete subject ("Why your beliefs are not yours") is the weakest shape there is.

## The rule under all of them
A hook promises; the clip must pay. Never write a hook Kevin does not deliver inside the clip.
Pick the pattern that matches the clip's psychological trigger. **Never use the same pattern
twice in one episode** - six clips should pull six different levers.

## The bank

**RESULT** - the thing they get
- `How rich people actually buy houses` (the 180K winner: a method, stated plainly)
- `What billionaires do differently`
- `The one thing that ends [problem]`
- `Three things that changed when he stopped [habit]`

**TIME** - speed or delay
- `How he made $1M before 18`
- `What 30 days of this did`
- `Why you're still broke after [years]`

**EFFORT** - it is easier than they fear
- `You don't need capital. You need this.`
- `No degree, no staff, no office`
- `It isn't discipline. It's a system.`

**CALLOUT** - names the exact person watching
- `If you're working and still broke`
- `For anyone who read every money book`
- `If you're over 30 with no savings`

**CONTRARIAN** - flips a belief they hold
- `Live within your means is a lie`
- `Positive thinking isn't enough`
- `Saving money keeps you poor`

**PAIN** - the cost of staying the same
- `Why your paycheck disappears`
- `The day he realised he was broke`
- `Busy is not the same as rich`

**MECHANISM** - the how, the system, the trick
- `The formula he used for $100K`
- `Three steps, in this order`
- `The question that exposes your problem`

**TRANSFORMATION** - the before and after
- `From prison to $100K months`
- `He lost $65M. Then this.`
- `Broke at 40, rich at 45`

**CURIOSITY** - the gap they must close
- `What nobody tells you about debt`
- `The one word that cost him millions`
- `What a bank teller told Ray Kroc`

**CONTROVERSY** - the polite lie, named
- `"Do what you love" is a trap`
- `Your mortgage was designed this way`
- `The advice that keeps you working`

## The story, inside the clip
The same arc decides WHERE a clip starts and ends:
**hook -> minimum context -> tension -> the turn -> the payoff.**
- **Start at the tension, not the beginning.** Cut the wind-up. A clip may start on the problem,
  the stranger's question, the number - never on Kevin clearing his throat toward a topic.
- **One open loop.** The first sentence should make the viewer need the last one.
- **Specific beats general** - a number, a name, a place. "$65 million overnight" holds; "a lot of
  money" does not. Prefer segments where Kevin names something real.
- **End on the turn**, the line that pays the promise. Never on a setup for the next question.

## The caption, same discipline
- The first line is all most people read: it must work alone, and it must NOT repeat the hook word for word.
- One idea per paragraph. Benefit before explanation. Contrast makes value visible (before/after, their way/his way).
- Answer the obvious objection inside the caption ("this only works with money to start" - say why it doesn't).
- **One** call to action, never two.
